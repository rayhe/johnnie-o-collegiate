Johnnie-O: How They Make College Apparel

A surf-meets-prep startup from 2005 that quietly built one of the biggest premium licensing machines in college sports, now 200+ schools deep.

The Brand

Johnnie-O was founded in 2005 by UCLA alumnus John O'Donnell, who fused East Coast prep with West Coast surf casual. The company is headquartered in Santa Monica, California, with a large presence in Raleigh, North Carolina.

Signature look: the four-button polo with the patented "Tweener Button," plus button-downs, swim, outerwear, and performance gear for men, women, and boys, priced roughly $50 to $130. The logo is a surfer silhouette with a longboard.

Where it sells: johnnie-o.com, more than 14 of its own stores, hundreds of specialty retailers, and thousands of golf and racquet clubs.

How the College Apparel Works

This is a licensing business, not a one-off collaboration. Johnnie-O signs official licensing deals with each school, then designs school-specific co-branded collections: polos, button-downs, outerwear, and swim carrying that school's marks, cut in Johnnie-O's own prep silhouettes.

"Dressing up fans, not dressing them down." John O'Donnell, founder, on the pitch: school pride without the face-paint fan gear
  • License the marks. Johnnie-O negotiates an official trademark license with the university, school by school. No license, no logos.
  • Design in-house. Designers build a collection around that school's colors and marks, but the garments keep Johnnie-O's prep silhouettes: the four-button polo, the button-down, the quarter-zip.
  • Manufacture to premium spec. The same supply chain and quality bar as the core line, which is why it prices like Johnnie-O ($50-130) instead of like stadium giveaway gear.
  • Distribute everywhere fans shop. Campus bookstores, johnnie-o.com, specialty retail, and the golf/racquet club channel where the brand already lives.

The Growth Story

2005

John O'Donnell founds Johnnie-O in California, blending prep and surf.

2016

First MLB license signed under Matt Ferrer (now company President). The licensing playbook begins.

2016-17

Collegiate apparel launches with roughly 30 schools, including Boston College, Bucknell, Georgetown, Vanderbilt, UVA, Wake Forest, Villanova, Northwestern, and Colgate.

2017-2024

Licensing expands across the NHL, PGA Tour, and USGA. Heath Hooper runs licensing as VP. Licensed sports apparel becomes a major growth driver.

2025

NFL deal signed, covering all 32 teams with 29 styles. College licenses pass 200 universities.
